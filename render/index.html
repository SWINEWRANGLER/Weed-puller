Weed Puller WP-1

A carry-sized robot that finds weeds by species, pulls them out root and all with a four-tine plunge head, then backfills the hole with sand the way a greenkeeper repairs a divot. It works inside a perimeter you set, stays out of the zones you mark, and maps the property on its first run.

The extraction cycle

The robot stops over each weed, pulls it, then rolls forward exactly 180 mm so the sand nozzle sits over the same hole. Nothing is dug and no soil is turned over.

  1. DetectThe down-looking camera under a shade hood with an LED ring gives even light at any time of day. An on-board model flags plants; species ID confirms the target.
  2. AlignThe crown is located to ±5 mm in the image and the robot creeps until the tine hub is centred over it.
  3. PlungeFour 8 mm stainless tines enter splayed, forming a 60 mm ring around the crown. Depth is set per species: 60 mm for clover, 120 mm for a dandelion taproot.
  4. Close & twistTines pivot inward under the root ball, the head turns 30° to shear fine roots, then lifts at 40 mm/s to avoid snapping the taproot.
  5. BinA wiper plate strips the plant into a sealed 6 L rear bin, so seeds don't drop back on the lawn.
  6. StepRTK and wheel odometry move the robot 180 mm forward, putting the nozzle over the hole.
  7. BackfillAn auger meters 15–30 ml of dry washed sand, scaled to plunge depth. Like a divot repair, it keeps the surface level and lets turf close over.
  8. LogEach pull is saved with GPS position, species and photo, building a weed-pressure heatmap over the seasons.

Systems

Vision & species ID

Two-stage, so it keeps working without signal in a back paddock.

  • On-board detector on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano: a YOLO-class model finds plants and marks weed vs. keep in real time. Fine-tuned on open weed datasets such as DeepWeeds, plus your own lawn.
  • Species confirmation through the Plant.id API (Kindwise), which identifies weeds from a photo with a confidence score. Pl@ntNet's API is the fallback. Calls are queued offline and synced later.
  • A keep-list protects natives, clover or anything else you choose.

Location & mapping

  • RTK-GNSS (u-blox ZED-F9P class) for ±2 cm positioning, with corrections from AUSCORS/NTRIP or a base station.
  • 2D LiDAR + IMU to trace fences, hedges and walls and pick up trees, tanks and sheds as obstacles.
  • The auto-map snaps to your cadastral parcel. It shows the fence line it found, but you confirm the boundary before the first job. A fence isn't always the title line.
  • Geofence: perimeter plus any number of exclusion zones. The robot stops if it drifts 0.3 m past an edge.

Soil care & transport

  • Low ground pressure: 18.5 kg on four 90 mm-wide low-pressure tyres, about 12 kPa. That's less than a person walking.
  • Small footprint: about 30 cm³ of soil displaced per pull, all of it replaced with sand. Nothing is turned over.
  • ATV-ready: two side carry handles, antenna mast folds flat, 850 × 610 mm sits crosswise on a standard rear rack. Strap points at all four corners.
  • Hot-swap 36 V 10 Ah pack, about 4 h per charge.
